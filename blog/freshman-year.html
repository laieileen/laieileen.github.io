Freshman Year @ CMU

They say time flies when you're having fun, so I must have had fun. My first semester seemed to be more like a typical college experience, whereas second semester was more a delicate balancing act of staying warm inside, studying for difficult classes, and still trying to socialize, but I think it being more hectic was actually still fun and formative!

Working Hard or Hardly Working?

In my fall semester, I took what I retrospectively call a "bummy schedule." Hindsight is 20/20 but I also do wonder if I would have been able to handle taking harder classes during my first semester as I adjusted to living on my own.

  • 36-202 Methods of Statistics and Data Science: The prof was delightful, his class was structured well, and I learned some statistics.
  • 21-127 Concepts of Math: As someone who has previously taken a high-quality discrete math class, this class was bad. The idea of the course and the concepts (haha) are what should be a college-level intro proof writing course. But the execution made me not like doing discrete math. The lectures were surface-level (e.g. you could just read the notes) such that I should have just skipped class instead of being responsible.
  • 76-101 Interpretation and Argument: What more could you ask from a first year writing course? This course neither exceeded nor underperformed my expectation.
  • 73-104 Intro to Microeconomics Accelerated: One of my fav professors! Game theory and auctions were more quantitative and interesting to me.
  • 21-295 Putnam Seminar: Very chill class.
  • 80-180 Intro to Linguistics: A peculiar class. The structure and organization wasn't great, but I found the first half up until the midterm to be somewhat fascinating. The second half we spent a long time on syntax and I didn't find this as interesting, although it did take me back to cases from Latin I.

In my second semester, I think I was already feeling less excited because the novelty of college had worn off and the weather for most of the semester was quite depressing.

  • 15-122 Principles of Imperative Programming: I could write a whole page about this class, but tl;dr the course content was engaging and fun, but the way it's tested was treacherous (but I understand why the class is structured so).
  • 73-103 Intro to Macroeconomics: I know many people thought this class was structured poorly, but I honestly enjoyed it since the professor explained everything well.
  • 21-241 Matrices & Linear Transformations: Woah this class was harder than expected content-wise. The content was cool to learn and the exams were moderately difficult, but I thoroughly enjoyed this class (what a good professor can do for a class!).
  • 66-155 Grand Challenge First-Year Seminar: Sports Betting, Highs and Lows: Your Brain on Stats: I enjoyed learning sports betting terminology and the psychology of it, but I thought the statistical modeling fell a bit short, but this makes sense as it's somewhat of an intro-level course. I also found the guest speakers (specifically Bill Benter) to be very knowledgeable.

The contrast between technicals and geneds was pretty extreme, but after my first year, I think highly of the CS classes and I'm somewhat looking forward to my sophomore year classes?

Boston, Wasabi, and Friends

One of the most unassuming clubs I joined was CMIMC. I originally went to the weekly math meetings to get free dinner, but luckily the company was good enough to keep me involved even when there was no free food involved.

The first major event I participated in was the HMMT volunteer exchange! My good friend hosted me at MIT, and I met up with some old friends who were volunteering or competing. I thoroughly enjoyed Boston, and I have to say it's much more fun than Pittsburgh.

Following HMMT volunteer exchange was our own competition, CMIMC! It was crazy to see how many students attended via the metric of Costco pizza. And the volunteer dinner had SO MUCH sushi. I've never seen this amount of sushi in one place before, and I definitely have not seen this much wasabi in just one box before.

This was the moment (maybe not seeing that amount of wasabi but rather the day as a whole) where I realized that 1. I have made some friends from this club and 2. it means a lot to me already. I think I wrote something about finding the community that makes me feel at home my college applications, and perhaps this is the one. Or maybe not. But the only way to find out is to keep chugging!

From Water to Land

Those who know me may know I used to do artistic (formerly synchronized) swimming in high school. Unfortunately, this sport is too niche, and thus, CMU does not have a team. Nonetheless, I occasionally practiced at the CMU pool, but I have some qualms with swimming at CMU:

  1. The times the pool is open are so sparse. On any given Tuesday, I can only swim from 7:00-9:00am. And as a typical college student I cannot wake up this early...
  2. Most of the time, Pittsburgh is cold and/or rainy, so I had to plan to shower and dry my hair and change at the pool (🫩🤯😫), rather than walking back to my dorm and doing all of the above in my dorm.
  3. Recreational swim lanes aren't too optimal for synchronized swimming, especially when sharing lanes. The silver lining, though, is that I made a lot of friends (who I will probably never meet again Sadge) from the intrigue of the bizarre sport that is artistic swimming.

I also tried new forms of fitness (namely pilates and weightlifting), but I had to take a short break when traveling home. Near the end of the first semester, I was studying for finals and going down the stairs of Gates and Hillman Center for dinner. I must have been reading something extremely riveting on my phone, for I wasn't focused on going down the stairs at all, and I missed the last one and twisted my ankle... And the next day, I limped to my linguistics final and ended up with an A in that class. Hooray?

Until Next Time

Thanks for reading! In the past year, I've become more interested in writing and speaking. I hope to create more and consume less, and writing blog posts is one step toward doing so. This post was not perfect by any means, but this has been sitting on my computer for a long time and I think it's time to free it from my computer jail. And in transparency, I think I've already forgotten everything that happened during my first year, and I have ideas for my future writing that aren't one year of living condensed into a single page. And so hopes sophomore year brings lots of fun new stories!
